AM, the core reasoner

AM's eyes measure every object in every frame in centimetres: Apple Vision tracks it, a metric depth model gives its distance, and a stated lens (60° wide) turns pixels into 3D.

AM then reasons over those numbers. For each object's own numbers (across, up and down, depth, width, height, brightness) its hives each propose a rule: EXACT, SHAPE, REPEAT or LOCAL. AM's law takes one and proves it on every frame, or answers CANNOT. A CANNOT is part of the answer: it marks what in the clip follows no law AM can stand behind.

From that map AM builds the scene as geometry, the surface, the camera and every piece, and works out the shot you ask for as numbers for every frame, locked before any generation.

Limits

  • Two shots so far, one passing take each. AM's eyes use an assumed lens and a depth model, not a calibrated camera.
  • When the prompt asks for something the scene makes implausible, video models improvise and ignore AM's numbers.
  • The guide drawing and the scoring are code written for these tests; the painting is the video model's.
  • The detector resolves about 2–3 px, and reads the pawn's head because its foot hides behind the pawn in front.

How AM maps, reasons and extends

  1. Eyes. Apple Vision boxes every object in every frame: centre x, y and size w, h in pixels.
  2. 3D lift. A stated lens, 60° wide, so f = (W/2) / tan 30° (740 px for an 854 px frame). Metric depth (Depth Anything V2 Metric) gives Z, the median depth of the box's inner 60%. Then X = (x − cx)·Z/f, Y = (y − cy)·Z/f, and the real size W = w·Z/f, H = h·Z/f, in centimetres.
  3. Shared memory. Six textured background patches, away from every object, are followed from the first frame and lifted the same way. Their median movement is the camera's track; their median brightness change is the exposure. Taking both away leaves each object's own numbers: rX = X − camera X (and so for Y and Z), W, H, and brightness oB.
  4. AM reasons. For each own number, AM's hives each propose a rule: EXACT (a chain of operations on the frame number t), SHAPE (a·s(t) + b), REPEAT (a table that repeats with a period) or LOCAL (a line through nearby frames). AM's number law reads what they report and names one, or answers CANNOT. The band τ = 3σ + 0.5 comes from the number's own frame-to-frame noise.
  5. The puzzle. The last frame as a board: the stage is the surface things stand on, fitted as a plane to the metric-depth points, which gives the camera's height and tilt above it. The pieces are the subject Apple Vision lifts out, plus everything standing up off the stage over its own footprint, cut apart where depth jumps; walls and cupboards are scenery. Each piece gets its place in stage coordinates (across, forward, up), its real size, what it rests on (the stage, another piece, or nothing), and what AM proved about it over the clip.
  6. The intent. What you want to happen is a question on that board: which piece goes where, how high, by when. Its answer is exact numbers for every new frame, turned back into boxes on screen by the lens (x = cx + f·X/Z, y = cy + f·Y/Z, w = f·W/Z, h = f·H/Z), and handed to the video model as a picture, which it follows; numbers in words it does not.
